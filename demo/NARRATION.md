# Why Math — restrained, expressive English voiceover

Version: 2026-11-09. Narration uses a balanced storytelling voice and is aligned to 72 seconds of fast-cut video (under the 90-second maximum).

| Time | Spoken narration |
|---|---|
| 00–09s | For years, students have had to adapt to the lesson. Memorize a formula. Follow the steps. Move on — even when it never clicks. |
| 09–18s | What if the lesson could adapt instead? Why Math begins teaching, listens to the student, and changes the next explanation. |
| 18–37s | Here are two simulated students. Both fourteen. Both starting from the same math idea. Student A needs the words explained. Student B has a different misunderstanding. One AI teacher responds to what each student actually says. It does not see their hidden test profiles. |
| 37–47s | The same teacher works in Korean, too. Questions are not failures. When a learner is unsure, the lesson explains rather than rushing ahead. |
| 47–55s | Some ideas are easier to see. Add an apple. Take it away. Now the basket is empty. That quantity has a name: zero. |
| 55–65s | Your Living Textbook grows automatically, saving the explanations, questions, and what still needs checking — not inventing mastery scores. |
| 65–72s | Same mathematics. Different minds. A path that adapts to you. Explore Why Math on GitHub. |

The students shown in the video are simulated test learners. No real-student learning outcomes are claimed.

Seven editable MP3 voice segments are stored in `demo/audio/`. The voiceover is not macOS TTS. The script can be used to generate an alternative voice if desired.
